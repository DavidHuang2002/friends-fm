# FriendsFM design contract

## Product

FriendsFM is a tiny nightly radio station between friends: one human recommendation, one song, one visual world, and one page. It removes choice instead of creating an infinite feed. The archive turns the year into a shared memory object.

The experience must feel like a digital postcard, not a playlist, dashboard, streaming clone, database, or AI demo.

## Stable anatomy

1. Borderless, full-viewport cover.
2. Small `FriendsFM!`, `How it works`, and `Archive` links without a conventional navbar.
3. Date, sequential postcard number, and sender.
4. Song title, artist, release context, and optional sender note.
5. A clear scroll or entry cue into an editorial story.
6. One or two concise listening-note paragraphs.
7. Official embedded player with visible controls.
8. Visible research sources.
9. Honest scheduled queue.
10. Clickable archive of real prior postcards.
11. Personal `What is this?` explanation and submission link.

## Voice

- Keep navigation, metadata labels, section titles, and process UI in English.
- Use the song's primary language for the listening note when that serves the audience.
- Preserve sender notes verbatim by default.
- Sound personal, concise, curious, and lightly informal.
- Avoid marketing language, inflated claims, and generic AI phrasing.
- Keep: `The pages are generated. The recommendations aren't.` unless David changes it.

## Visual system

- Derive a unique system from the recording's cover, era, physical texture, instrumentation, structure, or verified story.
- Extend the cover logic into the story section; do not switch to a generic article template.
- Use raster artwork for expressive imagery and CSS for composition, texture, rhythm, and simple shapes.
- Make each night materially different, not a color swap.
- Treat desktop and mobile as deliberate compositions.
- Use motion sparingly and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Maintain strong contrast and keep oversized type clear of labels and notes.

## Boundaries

- Link sources instead of making unsupported claims.
- Do not paste full lyrics or long copyrighted passages.
- Use official artwork only as contextual editorial artwork for its recording.
- Do not host copyrighted recordings.
- Label AI-generated artwork honestly in alt text or nearby context.
